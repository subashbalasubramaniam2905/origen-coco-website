From Every Coconut, Something Useful

From one idea to a global supplier

Origen Coco began with a simple thought: there should be a use for every part of a coconut.

There is much more to a coconut than the part we eat. The husk gives us fibre, pith, and chips, while the kernel can be dried to make copra. For years, some of these parts were discarded or sold for very little. We saw an opportunity to use them better, create useful products, and give local farmers a more dependable market for what they grow.

Around ten years ago, we began supplying coco peat blocks, coco husk chips, copra, coconut fibre, and coconuts across India. The business grew gradually, one order and one customer at a time. Many came back because they knew they could expect consistent products, reliable supply, and someone to speak to when they had a question.

Today, we supply growers, nurseries, and industries in overseas markets too. We carefully manage sourcing and processing, test our products, and align product preparation and quality checks with agreed buyer specifications. As the business has grown, the things that matter to us have stayed much the same: clear product information, straightforward communication, and reliable delivery.

Coco Peat Blocks

Washed, dried, and compressed coir pith that expands into a light, moisture-holding growing medium.

Coco peat is the fine, spongy pith left when coconut husk is separated into fibre. It is a natural, renewable alternative to peat moss that holds plenty of water without turning heavy or waterlogged.

At Origen Coco, we wash the pith to lower its salt content, dry it to the right moisture level, and press it into compact blocks that are easy to store, stack, and ship. Add water, and the block expands into a light, fluffy growing medium for bags, pots, trays and nursery mixes. Its compact form also makes it easier and more cost-effective to transport than loose coco peat.

Key benefits

  • Holds water well while draining freely
  • Renewable and moss-free
  • Compact blocks save on storage and freight space
  • Grows rapidly in wet conditions
  • Washed to reduce salt, with EC and pH kept in range
  • Can be reused across crops with proper care

Applications

  • Growing medium for vegetables, flowers and soft fruit in greenhouse and open fields
  • Seed starting and rooting of cuttings
  • Base in nursery and potting mixes
  • Soil conditioner for garden beds and containers

Suitable crops: Tomato, Cucumber, Pepper, Eggplant, Strawberry, Raspberry, Blueberry, Roses and other flowers

Coco Husk Chips

Diced husk pieces that keep roots well aerated and drain freely.

Coco husk chips are small pieces of coconut husk. They differ from coco peat, as they create a different planting medium that has more air pockets and drains excess water more effectively. This makes them a suitable choice for those plants that do best if their roots have air circulating around them.

Typically, growers use coco husk chips for growing orchids or combine them with coco peat. A grower can select the size of the chips, depending on the plant and container.

Key benefits

  • High air space around roots
  • Fast, even drainage
  • Breaks down slowly, so the mix stays open longer
  • Natural and renewable
  • Suits plants that dislike wet roots

Applications

  • Orchid cultivation
  • Blending with coco peat to improve aeration
  • Potting mixes for large containers and nurseries
  • Mulch for garden beds

Coconut Fibre

Strong, natural fibre from the husk for industrial use.

Coconut fibre is the long, strong strand found inside the husk. It is tough, resistant to wear and fully natural. We take the same husk that gives us coco peat, separate the fibre from the pith, clean it, and press it into bales that are simple to handle and ship.

Buyers turn it into mats, rope and brushes, erosion-control nets, garden products and upholstery filling, often in place of synthetic fibres. If you need a particular length, grade, or bale size, we can discuss what is possible for your order.

Key benefits

  • Strong and long-lasting
  • Natural and biodegradable
  • Made from the same husk that gives us coco peat
  • Suits many industrial uses

Applications

Copra is the dried kernel of the coconut and the main raw material for coconut oil. Its quality depends on the coconuts and proper drying of copra, which we closely monitor.

From Farm to Final Shipment

How We Prepare Our Products

A structured process from sourcing and production to quality checks and shipment. The steps vary by product, but the focus remains on consistent quality and careful handling at every stage.

ICoco peat blocks and husk chips

  1. 01

    Sourcing from local farmers

    We buy coconuts and husks directly from local growers. Each lot is checked for quality when it arrives, and anything that does not meet our standards is set aside.

  2. 02

    Separating and sieving

    The husk is separated into fibre, pith, and chips. The pith and chips are then sieved to the required size, while oversized pieces and unwanted material are removed.

  3. 03

    Washing

    The pith is washed with clean water to reduce its salt content. We check the EC and pH before it moves on to make sure it is suitable for growing applications.

  4. 04

    Drying

    The washed material is dried to the required moisture level and turned during the process so it dries evenly. Moisture is checked before the material moves to the next stage.

  5. 05

    Compressing and packing

    The dried peat is pressed into blocks according to the required size and weight. The blocks are then packed, stacked, and secured on pallets for storage and transport.

  6. 06

    Quality check

    Samples are checked against the product specifications, including EC, pH, moisture, and block weight. Only batches that meet the requirements are cleared for dispatch, with records kept for traceability.

  7. 07

    Shipping

    The finished pallets are loaded into containers and secured for transport. Shipments travel by road and sea, and customers are kept informed about dispatch and delivery.

IICopra

Copra follows its own process, from the coconut on the farm to the bag in the container. Here is how we prepare it.

  1. Collect

    We source mature coconuts directly from local growers and check them for quality when they arrive. Buying from farmers also allows us to trace each lot back to its source.

  2. Dry

    The kernel is removed from the shell and dried to the required moisture level. Careful drying helps prevent mould and spoilage, and the moisture is checked before the copra moves on.

  3. Sort and pack

    The dried copra is checked and graded, with pieces that do not meet our standards removed. It is then packed in bags to protect it from moisture before being prepared for shipment.

IIICoconuts

Whole coconuts are graded and packed with the same care. Here is how we handle them from the farm to the container.

  1. Collect

    We source mature coconuts directly from local growers and check them when they arrive. Damaged or immature nuts are set aside before the coconuts move to grading.

  2. Grade

    The coconuts are sorted by size and quality to keep each lot consistent. Buyers can specify the size range that best suits their market, and the graded nuts are checked again before packing.

  3. Pack and ship

    The coconuts are packed to help prevent damage during transit and checked before leaving our unit. They are then loaded securely for transport by road and sea.

How to Use Coco Peat and Husk Chips

Find practical help here on expanding coco peat blocks, using husk chips for orchids and looking after your growing media.

How to expand a coco peat block?

  1. 1Place the block in a clean bucket, tub, or tray large enough for it to swell.
  2. 2Pour in clean water slowly.
  3. 3Wait 15 to 20 minutes while the block absorbs the water and softens.
  4. 4Break up the block with your hands or a fork until it is loose and fluffy.
  5. 5If the peat is unwashed, or you grow salt-sensitive crops, rinse it and check EC before use.
  6. 6Fill pots, bags or trays and plant.

How to use husk chips for orchids?

  1. 1Pick a chip size that suits your orchid's roots. Larger chips suit larger plants.
  2. 2Soak the chips in clean water for a few hours, then drain.
  3. 3Add a layer of chips to the pot, set the orchid in, and fill around the roots.
  4. 4Water when the chips feel dry. Let extra water drain out fully.
  5. 5Replace the chips every one to two years, or when they start to break down.

FAQs

Can I reuse coco peat?
In many cases, yes. Remove old roots and debris, rinse out built-up salts and add fresh nutrients before replanting. If the last crop had a disease problem, start with new material.
Does coco peat need feeding?
Yes. Coco peat holds very few nutrients on its own, so use a balanced fertiliser programme that includes calcium and magnesium. Check the EC and pH of your feed regularly.
How much growing medium does one block make?
It varies by the block’s dimensions and quality. A dried block expands considerably when it soaks up the moisture, and the expansion capacity can be checked on the specification table. Tell us the dimensions of your blocks, and we can assist you with the calculation of the number of blocks you will require.
Can I mix husk chips with coco peat?
Yes. Coco peat holds moisture, while husk chips add air space and fast drainage. Use more chips for plants that dislike wet roots, and more peat for crops that need steady moisture.
How should I store blocks?
Store them in a dry place, away from the floor and direct sunlight, and keep the packaging intact until you are ready to use them.
